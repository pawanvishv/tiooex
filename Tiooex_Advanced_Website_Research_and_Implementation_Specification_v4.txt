Primary objective: turn Tiooex into a premium, credible, easy-to-evaluate enterprise technology website that generates qualified enquiries without overstating capabilities.

# 1. Executive recommendation

The previous audit correctly identified broad positioning, limited proof, unclear product maturity, insufficient service detail and a weakly evidenced conversion path. After reviewing the actual live page content and the previous document, the recommendations can now be more specific.

Tiooex has a stronger editorial foundation than a generic IT services brochure: its homepage describes delivery constraints, rollback points, data ownership, human review and security release gates. Its platform pages describe operational workflows. Preserve that differentiated, problem-led voice. The main weakness is that the visitor is not yet shown enough evidence, product UI, team credibility, detailed scope or a frictionless next step.

Recommended direction: a calm, premium enterprise design with real product demonstrations, concise positioning, use-case-led navigation, clear evidence and layered calls to action. Use Parallel.ai as a benchmark for how to explain a complex technology proposition—not as a design to copy.

Priority: fix the buyer journey and content architecture first; then add product evidence and case studies; then polish visual design, SEO and technical quality.

# 5. Positioning: decide what Tiooex wants to be known for

Current homepage language—“Reliable Technology For Business” and “Transform operations and accelerate growth across every industry”—is too broad to differentiate the company. The footer also says “robotic machines, telecom, and more”, which conflicts with the more focused cloud/data/security story and should be removed unless these are current, clearly described offerings.

## Recommended positioning statement

Build, modernise and run the systems your business depends on.

Tiooex helps businesses engineer secure digital platforms, connect critical workflows and modernise operations through software engineering, cloud, data and AI.

Suggested CTAs: Primary — “Discuss a project”; Secondary — “Explore platforms”. Use only if this statement accurately reflects current delivery capability.

## Choose three strategic pillars

| Pillar | What belongs here | Buyer outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digital Engineering | Custom software, platform modernisation, integration, APIs, application engineering. | Systems that are easier to change, integrate and scale. |
| Cloud, Data & AI | Cloud foundations, DevOps, data engineering, analytics, AI/automation, security engineering. | Reliable infrastructure and better operational decisions. |
| Business Platforms | Commerce, Mobility, Banking & Insurance products/solutions—only those that are active and supportable. | Connected business workflows with a clear operating model. |

Do not present every capability as equally important. Select the 3–5 services that Tiooex can sell and deliver confidently today. Other capabilities can remain as supporting or partner-led offerings if their status is clear.

# 7. Page-by-page redesign specification

1. Engagement models: Advisory, Sprint, Managed, Partnership with outcomes and suitable use cases.

1. Actual UI screenshots (dashboard, core workflow, reports, mobile/tablet views).

Live page covers Automotive, Banking, Capital markets, Insurance, Retail, Industrial, Energy, Health and Public service. The descriptions are thoughtful but short. The homepage's featured use case is Automotive, so the page should not promise nine deep vertical solutions unless the company has the knowledge and proof.

Prioritise four industries that align to the platform and delivery strengths: Automotive & Mobility, Retail & Commerce, Banking & Insurance, and Industrial & Energy. Keep other industries in a secondary “Additional sectors” section until the content is credible.

### Industry detail page template

1. Industry-specific opening problem and business context.

1. Who the page is for: COO, CIO, CTO, operations leader, product owner, risk/compliance leader.

1. 3–5 common operational challenges.

1. One end-to-end workflow map.

1. Relevant Tiooex platform and service mapping.

1. Reference implementation or approved case study.

1. FAQs and industry-specific CTA.

Example: Automotive should show vehicle fault → connected event → triage → workshop assignment → parts/repair → closure → fleet/OEM reporting. This is more persuasive than a general statement about connected vehicles.

## 7.5 Insights and resources

Live page has six short notes and explicitly says they are perspectives, not client results. That transparency is good. The next step is to make them useful, substantive resources.

- Create a full URL for each article with author, publication date, updated date, reading time, summary, headings, diagrams and references.

- Write for a named buyer question: e.g. “How to modernise a legacy commerce platform without disrupting POS operations”.

- Link each article to one relevant service, platform and case study.

- Add a practical asset: checklist, architecture diagram, decision framework or downloadable guide.

- Hide empty category states or show a helpful “Explore all insights” link.

- Publish only at a cadence the team can maintain. Two strong articles each quarter are better than many thin posts.

- Keep the disclaimer that thought leadership is not a client result where relevant.

Suggested first 8 article topics:

- How to plan a phased cloud migration with rollback points.

- How to connect e-commerce, POS and inventory without stock mismatch.

- From vehicle fault to workshop closure: designing a connected mobility workflow.

- Human-in-the-loop AI: ownership, review and auditability in enterprise workflows.

- Security gates that should exist before production release.

- How to measure operational workflow automation beyond number of dashboards.

## 7.6 Contact / Start a project

Create a dedicated /contact/ or /start-a-project/ page even if the header Contact currently opens an email client. Email-only contact creates friction, offers no context and is difficult to measure.

- Form fields: name, work email, company, role (optional), service/platform interest, project stage, message and consent/privacy acknowledgement.

- Use a two-step form only if the first step is short and the second step adds useful qualification. Avoid demanding budget or phone number too early.

- Offer three clear routes: Discuss a project; Request a platform demo; Partnership enquiry.

- Show a verified business email, phone if staffed, office location and expected response time.

- On submission: server-side validation, spam/rate protection, acknowledgement email, internal routing, CRM or secure inbox handoff, success state and error recovery.

- Track form start, completion, validation failure, CTA click and booked meeting.

Add an About page so enterprise buyers can understand who they are evaluating.

- Leadership and delivery team profiles with real names, roles and professional backgrounds.

- Delivery locations, legal entity details and company contact information.

## Recommended homepage visual composition

Desktop: left side contains the H1, two-line explanation and CTAs; right side shows a real platform dashboard or workflow visual in a framed product window. Beneath it, a slim proof row. Use alternating light/dark sections to create rhythm. Mobile: stack the copy first, then a compressed product visual, then CTAs; do not hide the main message inside a carousel.

## Motion and interaction ideas worth building

- Interactive workflow explorer: select a platform or industry and reveal steps, users, systems and outputs.

- Product screenshot gallery with zoom and annotated callouts.

- Short product demo video with captions and poster image; click-to-play rather than autoplay by default.

- Before/after process comparison with a clear explanation of what changed.

- Sticky in-page navigation on long service/product pages.

- Searchable capability directory with accessible filters.

- Subtle hover states and scroll reveals that never block reading or delay content.

# 10. SEO, content and discoverability

- Create unique, descriptive page titles and meta descriptions for every indexable page.

- Use one clear H1 per page, descriptive H2 sections and human-readable URLs.

- Make important service and product details available in crawlable HTML, not only in JavaScript-only modal content.

- Create XML sitemap, robots.txt, canonical URLs, Open Graph metadata and breadcrumb navigation.

- Add structured data only when accurate and eligible: Organization, BreadcrumbList, Article, Product or SoftwareApplication as appropriate. Do not add fake ratings or reviews.

- Write original, helpful content answering actual buyer questions; avoid keyword stuffing and generic AI-generated filler.

- Build internal links between service pages, industry pages, platform pages, case studies and relevant insights.

- Keep the mobile page content, metadata, headings and structured data equivalent to desktop.

- Use descriptive alt text for informative visuals; decorative images should have empty alt text.

- Set up Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools; submit sitemap and monitor indexing, queries and page experience.

- Prepare redirect mapping before changing URLs; monitor 404s and lost organic landing pages after launch.

Keyword direction (validate with keyword research before targeting): enterprise software development company, cloud migration services, application modernisation, data engineering services, AI workflow automation, cybersecurity consulting, retail POS and inventory platform, fleet management software, banking workflow automation, insurance claims management software. Use only the terms matching actual offers.

Google guidance: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide and https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing.

# 11. Performance, accessibility, security and engineering requirements

## Performance

- Measure real mobile and desktop performance before and after redesign; do not assume a problem without a measurement.

- Target Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile: LCP ≤ 2.5 seconds, INP ≤ 200 ms and CLS ≤ 0.1.

- Use responsive AVIF/WebP images with width/height attributes; lazy-load below-the-fold media; prioritise the hero asset.

- Minimise JavaScript, avoid unnecessary animation libraries and defer non-critical scripts.

- Use font subsets/preloading carefully, cache static assets and compress responses.

- Measure using PageSpeed Insights, Lighthouse and Search Console field data.

## Accessibility

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA as the practical design/development standard.

- Keyboard-only test all navigation, tabs, filters, dialogs, galleries and forms.

- Ensure focus is visible and not trapped; dialogs return focus to the trigger.

- Use semantic buttons and links, proper labels, descriptive accessible names and helpful validation errors.

- Ensure text contrast, zoom/reflow, reduced motion and captions/transcripts for videos.

## Security and privacy

- HTTPS everywhere, security headers, secure cookie practices and dependency patching.

- Validate and sanitise all form inputs server-side; rate-limit form submissions and protect against abuse.

- Do not expose API keys, environment variables, internal endpoints or sensitive data in client-side code.

- Publish an accurate privacy policy and explain how enquiry information is used and retained.

- Only claim certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, HIPAA etc.) if Tiooex has verified evidence and permission to make the claim.

- Document actual access control, encryption, backups, incident response and data retention practices before describing them publicly.

Core Web Vitals reference: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals

# 13. Content proof: what Tiooex must collect internally

The biggest barrier to making the site feel like a top-tier enterprise company is not visual design; it is the availability of real evidence. Before writing claims, collect:

- Approved customer names/logos or permission for anonymised descriptions.

- Project challenge, solution, technologies, timeline, team role and measured outcomes.

- Real screenshots from the platforms using synthetic/demo data that contains no customer personal information.

- Product status: live, pilot, beta, concept or planned; who supports it; current integrations; deployment options.

- Leadership/team biographies and actual delivery credentials.

- Partner agreements and permission to use partner branding.

- Security policies, architecture facts and certifications that can be substantiated.

- Support hours, SLA, response process, escalation path and geography actually served.

If the company cannot provide a proof point, do not fill the gap with a fabricated metric. Use a clearly labelled reference architecture, sample workflow or product concept.

# 20. Technical architecture recommendation

Do not select a framework because a reference website uses it. Select the simplest maintainable architecture that fits Tiooex's content publishing needs, interactive experiences, SEO requirements, security model and team skills. The public audit cannot identify the current source stack, hosting or CMS; these recommendations are a target architecture, not a diagnosis of the existing code.

## 20.1 Architecture principles

- Content-first and SEO-friendly: critical page content must be present in server-rendered HTML or equivalent crawlable output.

- Progressive enhancement: pages remain readable and navigable if optional JavaScript or animation fails.

- Design-system driven: shared tokens and components prevent each page becoming a one-off.

- Composable page templates: service, platform, industry, case study and article pages use reusable templates and structured content.

- Security by default: validate input on the server, protect secrets, rate-limit public forms and minimise third-party scripts.

- Performance budgets: every new dependency, animation, video and image must justify its cost.

- Operational visibility: logs, uptime checks, error monitoring, analytics and deployment rollback are planned from the start.

## 20.2 Reference application layers

| Layer | Recommended responsibility | Implementation notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Presentation | Responsive site shell, navigation, page templates, design system, accessible interactions. | Use semantic HTML, CSS variables/tokens, small client-side islands only where interactivity is needed. |
| Content | Pages, service records, product records, industries, case studies, articles, FAQs, navigation. | Use a headless CMS or a well-governed existing CMS; define editorial roles and preview workflow. |
| Lead capture | Contact, demo and project enquiry forms. | Server-side validation, spam controls, consent record, notification, CRM/secure inbox integration. |
| Integration | CRM, scheduling, email, analytics, consent manager and optional demo tooling. | Use approved vendor APIs, environment-managed secrets and clear data-processing agreements. |
| Delivery | Build, test, deploy, monitor, backup and rollback. | CI checks, staging environment, release approvals, uptime/error monitoring and documented rollback. |

## 20.3 Stack selection guidance

| Need | Suitable direction | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketing site with many content pages | Static generation or server rendering with a CMS. | Choose if content SEO, fast page delivery and editorial publishing dominate. |
| Highly interactive product demos | Small isolated React/TypeScript components or a dedicated demo tool. | Use only for real interactions; do not ship a large app bundle for decorative effects. |
| Existing Laravel/PHP website | Retain Laravel if it is secure, maintainable and the team can support it; improve templates and asset pipeline. | Avoid a full rewrite unless there is a measurable limitation in the current platform. |
| CMS/editorial workflow | Existing CMS or headless CMS with preview, role-based publishing and structured content. | Editors should update content without developer deployment. |
| Forms and CRM | Server-side endpoint with validated CRM integration. | Do not send sensitive enquiry data to client-side analytics or expose API credentials. |

Architecture decision record (ADR) required before selecting or changing the framework: context, options considered, constraints, security/privacy impact, performance impact, team capability, operating cost, migration risk, chosen option and review date.

# 21. Content model and CMS schema

Create structured content types rather than hard-coding all content into page components. This improves reuse, internal linking, preview and governance.

| Content type | Required fields | Relationships |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service | Name, slug, summary, buyer problem, outcomes, scope, deliverables, technologies, process, FAQs, CTA, status, SEO title/description. | Related industries, platforms, case studies, insights. |
| Platform | Name, status (live/pilot/beta/concept), target users, modules, workflows, screenshots, demo URL, integrations, deployment, security, support, FAQs. | Related industries, services, case studies. |
| Industry | Name, market context, buyer roles, challenges, workflows, integrations, risks, relevant services/platforms, proof. | Related case studies and insights. |

# 22. Advanced UX and interaction specification

## 22.1 Navigation behaviour

- Desktop: sticky header with a clear active state; dropdowns open on click and keyboard, not hover alone.

- Mobile: accessible drawer; lock background scroll while open; Escape closes; focus returns to menu trigger.

- Every menu item is a real link. Do not use clickable divs for navigation.

- Use breadcrumbs on deep pages and contextual in-page navigation for long service/product pages.

- Provide a visible route back to the main product/service directory.

## 22.2 Interactive industry explorer

1. Visitor selects an industry.

1. Show a specific operational challenge and the persona affected.

1. Show a 4–7 step workflow map with inputs, systems, decisions and outputs.

1. Map the workflow to one relevant Tiooex platform and one service line.

1. Show a real proof item or clearly labelled reference implementation.

1. Offer a contextual CTA that carries the selected industry into the enquiry form.

## 22.3 Product demo design

- Start with a guided 60–120 second demo path for one high-value workflow.

- Use synthetic records and a visible demo-data label; never expose live customer data.

- Allow restart, skip and keyboard operation; show progress and estimated duration.

- Each step should explain the user action and the business consequence, not only point at a button.

- At the end, offer a relevant CTA: request a tailored demo, discuss integration or download a product brief.

- Provide a static screenshot/video alternative for low-bandwidth users and assistive technology.

- Track demo started, step completed, demo completed and CTA clicked; do not collect unnecessary personal data.

## 22.4 Form UX

- Ask only for information needed to route the enquiry. Name, work email, company, area of interest and message are a reasonable first step.

- Make phone, budget and detailed project scope optional unless there is a clear business need.

- Explain why a field is requested; mark required and optional fields clearly.

- Preserve valid fields when validation fails; show errors beside the relevant field and in an accessible summary.

- Provide a clear confirmation and expected response time; do not promise a response SLA the team cannot meet.

- Record consent and privacy notice version when required; separate marketing subscription consent from responding to an enquiry.

# 23. Design tokens and component library specification

# 25. Conversion optimisation programme

Do not set arbitrary conversion targets before collecting a baseline. Run controlled experiments on high-traffic, high-intent pages; document hypothesis, segment, primary metric, guardrail metric, sample size approach, start/end dates and decision.

| Experiment | Hypothesis | Primary metric | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero headline A/B | A specific buyer/outcome statement helps visitors understand the offer faster than a generic slogan. | Primary CTA click-through and qualified enquiry rate. | Bounce/engagement and lead quality. |
| CTA wording | “Discuss a project” may work better for services while “Request a demo” works better for products. | CTA click-to-completion rate. | Form abandonment. |
| Product visual | Real UI screenshot with annotation may build more confidence than abstract illustration. | Platform detail CTA rate. | Page speed and mobile layout. |
| Short guided demo | A short workflow demo may reduce uncertainty before a demo request. | Demo engagement/completion and downstream meeting rate. | Load time and accessibility. |
| Proof placement | A relevant case study near a service CTA may improve confidence. | Qualified form submissions per service-page visit. | Page length and scroll depth. |
| Form simplification | Removing nonessential fields may increase completion without lowering lead quality. | Form completion rate. | Sales-qualified lead rate. |
| Industry-specific CTA | Carrying industry context into the enquiry may improve routing and relevance. | Industry page enquiry rate. | Lead routing accuracy. |

Navattic's 2026 benchmark report defines demo engagement as users progressing beyond the first step, completion as users finishing the first flow, and CTA CTR as users clicking through after engagement. Adopt consistent definitions so the team can compare demos over time. Do not use its top-percentile numbers as a forecast for Tiooex.

# 28. Advanced QA and release strategy

| Test layer | Required checks | Release gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Functional | All links, routes, menus, filters, accordions, forms, demo controls, empty/error/success states. | No critical or high-severity broken journey. |
| Responsive | Small mobile, large mobile, tablet, laptop and wide desktop; no overflow; readable forms and diagrams. | All priority pages pass visual review. |
| Accessibility | Keyboard-only, focus order, contrast, headings, labels, dialog focus, zoom/reflow, reduced motion, screen reader spot checks. | No known critical WCAG 2.2 AA blockers; documented exceptions. |
| Performance | Lighthouse lab checks plus field data where available; image/font/JS budgets; CWV monitoring. | No regression against agreed budgets; LCP/INP/CLS targets tracked. |
| SEO | Status codes, canonical, metadata, sitemap, robots, structured data validation, redirects, noindex rules. | No accidental noindex, broken canonicals or redirect loops. |
| Security | Dependency scan, secret scan, form abuse tests, header review, access review. | No unresolved critical/high vulnerabilities. |
| Content | Claims, spelling, link destinations, image rights, metadata, CTA context and approval. | All public claims approved and traceable. |
| Analytics | Events fire once, correct properties, consent behaviour, no PII in payloads. | Test events visible and validated. |

- Maintain local, staging and production environments; never test new forms against live customer data.

- Use preview deployments for content/design review.

- Run automated checks in CI: lint, typecheck (if applicable), unit tests, link checks, accessibility smoke tests, dependency scan and build.

- Use a staged rollout: internal review → limited production release → full release after monitoring.

- Keep a rollback plan and preserve old content/redirects until launch is stable.
